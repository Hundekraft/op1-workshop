About this manual

This manual covers what hundekraft adds to the original OP-1. That is a lot: the hundekraft synths and effects, chord mode, op1r's synths, effects, drum+, sequencers, quad LFO, sample editor, tape and system additions, ultralazr's Musica Obscura sounds, the storage, naming, scale and undo tools after Maiko Pruett's firmware, four tapes, tape edits, sampler tools and a MIDI sequencer.

Links from the workshop show the manual for your selected plugins. Readable URLs list the plugins you left out, for example manual.html?version=3.0&without=sid,oxbow#father. They work in another browser too. Choose Show everything to see all sections; a plain manual.html link opens the full manual. Encoded selection links are no longer supported; open the full manual or follow a new link from the workshop.

Stock OP-1 features (tape, mixer, album, the factory synths and effects) are not repeated here. Teenage Engineering's own guide covers them well: teenage.engineering/guides/op-1/original. Where a new feature sits on top of a stock one, you get a sentence of context and a link.

How to read the controls

The OP-1 has four encoders. This manual calls them by colour, left to right: blue, green, white and orange. Some older op1r notes call the fourth one red. It's the same knob.

  • Turn an encoder to change a value. Push an encoder to press it like a button.
  • Press a key, or hold it while you do something else. "Shift + " means hold Shift and press .
  • to are the four keys under the screen. COM is the album key at the top right (COM is its Shift function), the sequencer key, and , and the three tape-edit keys.
  • Sound keys 1 to 8 are the eight small numbered keys to the right of , under the encoders. They store and load sounds.

The key screens have numbered dots. The list under the picture says what each number shows. Dot colour matches the encoder that moves that part.

What's new

The current release is 3.1. This list starts with what it adds, then what came with 3.0.

New in 3.1

  • New synths, effects and a sequencer: MONOLIT, a bass and lead synth with a ladder filter, outatime, a time-travel synth, Bloodmoon, a gothic organ with a bell that never quite agrees with it, SISTER, a cathedral reverb whose tail sings, Grids (a map of drum patterns), Brassophone (a brass horn), dddd (harsh noise), Steve (Steve Reich's phasing as an effect), Nostromo (a plate reverb with a clock), JUNI (an early-80s polysynth), JUNI chorus (its stereo chorus), Hollow Saint (a haunted hurdy-gurdy), blot (a TB-303 style bass) and VAPORA (drifting pads).
  • New screens: JUNI, JUNI chorus, Wavetable and Taffy have new pictures. JUNI chorus had none before.
  • Pew, redesigned. It has a jazz-club screen, four encoders that each work like a whole organ control (drawbars, percussion, chorus and drive, the rotary speaker), and 16 new presets. Pew sounds saved before 3.1 play differently, because the encoders mean other things now. See Pew.
  • Father is lighter. The controls are the same, but the room underneath was rebuilt and needs much less CPU. Its long tail is a little darker, and a sound saved with Father may ring a little differently. See Father.
  • Amp has five voices (Clean, Crunch, Lead, Bass, Fuzz) and a needle tuner. Drive now runs through an amp and speaker model with a noise gate, so sounds saved with more Drive will sound different. See Amp.
  • More from op1r: master effect routing with sends from the tape, a sample browser in Granular, play mode and portamento in sampler+, pentatonic and blues plus four key modes in scale mode, and a second chord page with voicings and strum (page B).
  • HAIL moves Pitch in semitones. Hold Shift while turning for cents.
  • Resource monitor has two new pages: the slowest audio block and the shortest gap between blocks. See Resource monitor.
  • Less crackle. Before the audio runs late, the OP-1 now silences synth voices one at a time instead of crackling. Many engines and effects also need less CPU and sound the same: Wavetable, Elg, Brassophone and Father about a third of before, Pew, Taffy and Granular under half, Clouds, Reverb and Nostromo about half. MONOLIT, outatime, EVA, Oxbow, MBIRA, SISTER, Clouds, Nostromo and Palme now need less CPU again and sound the same. Plugins also live in faster memory now, away from the screen's. Their slowest moments take about half as long, so full chords on heavy synths play cleanly.
  • Fixes: disk mode ends again when you eject on the computer or press COM. Tape 1 records even when flash is low. The OP-1 starts even with more sampler sounds than it has room for.
  • Install the full firmware once when you move up from 3.0 (boot menu, or the workshop's full update). Nothing is formatted, but back up your tapes and sounds first. After that, updates go straight over USB.

Added with 3.0

Gin adds optional drum synthesis: 24 sounds per kit, per-sound A/B morphing, a full editor and eight factory kits. It keeps four synthesis voices and appears alongside DBOX and DRUM+. See Gin.

  • Send updates over USB. After the first full install, the workshop sends new builds straight to the OP-1, and it can add, update or remove single plugin files without a whole firmware update. You can also add your own plugins. See Send it over USB.
  • New synths: EVA, a space choir over the radio, MBIRA, a thumb piano, Oxbow, an OB-style polysynth with normal OP-1 presets, and SID, an 8580-inspired chip synth.
  • New effects: STUTTER, a stutter locked to the beat, HAIL, clouds of grains, plus Maiko’s Crush, Warble and Plate, and Father, an original stereo ambience reverb.
  • Aether and sampler+: a tilt theremin and expanded synth sampler. See Aether and Sampler+.
  • Randomiser: make variations with Shift + , step back with Shift + . See Randomiser.
  • More controls: smooth the loop join in In a room, and view audio, voice and plugin details in the resource monitor. See Clouds, Reverb, In a room and Resource monitor.
  • C64 arpeggiator: an editable pitch table with per-step dwell and gate. See C64 arpeggiator.
  • Four tapes, each with its own tracks, loop, tempo and album. See Four tapes.
  • Tape edits to build songs while they play: merge, copy the loop, paste after it, bounce. See Tape edits.
  • Undo, seven steps back, now with a popup. See Undo and redo.
  • Sampler tools: zoom, a tuner with retune, and fit a sample to 1 to 8 bars. See Sampler tools.
  • Storage tools rebuilt: FILE, now with a flash check, and a fix that stops recording from wrongly retiring good flash. See Safer recording.
  • MIDI sequencer: record notes and CCs on four tracks that play with the tape, plus CC control of the synth's knobs. See MIDI sequencer.
  • Safer starts. Plugin files that fail their loading checks are skipped. See Go back, or get unstuck.

Quick start

This chapter takes you from nothing to a working hundekraft OP-1 with presets. Most of the time goes to waiting while the OP-1 installs.

What you need

  • An original OP-1. Not the OP-1 field: this firmware does not run on it.
  • A USB cable and a computer with a current browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox or Edge).
  • Teenage Engineering's unmodified firmware file op1_246.op1 (R246). The workshop checks its fingerprint and refuses anything else, including op1r and other patched files.
  • Optional: a backup of your tapes and sounds. Updating keeps them, but a backup is cheap.

Build your firmware

The workshop builds the firmware in your browser. Nothing is uploaded and you don't need an account.

  1. Open the workshop at hundekraft.github.io/op1-workshop.
  2. Choose what you want. The tabs sort everything by kind: synths, effects, play and sequence, tape and sampler, system and storage. Cards with a box are ticked by default; untick one to leave it out. Cards without a box are always in. Each card has a link to its page in this manual.
  3. Your build has room for 300 user synth presets (the stock OP-1 has 100). With every sound selected, the preset pack has 180.
  4. Drop op1_246.op1 on the page, or choose it. The page checks the file and says so when it's the right one.
  5. Press "Build firmware file". The first build downloads the Python runtime the builder uses, so it takes a little longer.
  6. When the page says "Verified. Your firmware is ready to download.", download the firmware (.op1) and the matching preset pack (.presets.zip).

The firmware file is named hundekraft- plus the version. The matching build report (.build.json) lists the plugins you chose; keep it if you need to know later what a build contains.

Tip

"Save choices" stores your selection as a small file. Load it next time to build the same firmware again, for example for a new release.

Start clean

Coming from another firmware, such as op1r or an older custom build? Start with a clean drive. Leftover files from other firmwares can trip up a new one, and a format takes them all away. Updating hundekraft over hundekraft doesn't need this.

  1. Back up. On the OP-1, hold Shift and press COM, then press for disk mode. Copy the TAPE, ALBUM, DRUM and SYNTH folders to your computer, then eject.
  2. Format. Switch the OP-1 off. Hold COM and switch it on to open the boot menu, then choose 8, Format drive. This erases everything on the OP-1, the firmware too.
  3. Install. In the same boot menu, choose 1, Upload firmware, and install your hundekraft file as described below.
  4. Copy back. When the OP-1 has started, go to disk mode again and copy your folders back.

Install it on the OP-1

This is the OP-1's normal firmware update. It keeps your tapes, album and sounds.

  1. Switch the OP-1 off. Hold COM and switch it on. The firmware update menu appears.
  2. Press to choose "Upload firmware".
  3. The screen asks you to connect the OP-1 to a computer. Plug in the USB cable; the OP-1 appears as a disk.
  4. Copy the hundekraft .op1 file to that disk.
  5. Eject the disk on the computer, or press COM. The OP-1 unpacks and writes the firmware. This takes a few minutes.
  6. When the screen says "Please restart your OP-1", switch it off and on again.

The boot screen shows "hundekraft" and the version number. That's how you know it worked.

Next time is easier. Once hundekraft is on your OP-1, plug in USB in the workshop and press Connect my OP-1. Updates and new plugins go straight over, with no file and no boot menu. See Send it over USB.

Send it over USB

Once your OP-1 runs hundekraft, you don't need the update menu anymore: the workshop sends new builds straight to the OP-1 over USB. The first install still goes through the update menu.

  1. Choose the plugins you want in the workshop and connect the OP-1 by USB. Use Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer, and allow MIDI access when asked.
  2. Press Connect my OP-1 to see what is installed, or Review changes to check and prepare changes in one go. Check again reads the installed plugins once more. Cancel connection drops a pending MIDI permission request without sending anything.
  3. Look through the proposed additions, replacements, removals and on/off changes. Unchanged plugins are folded away. If two plugins use the same controls, pick the one that stays on. Then press Send to OP-1.
  4. Keep the cable connected. When the page says Confirm on OP-1, press on the instrument to restart and install, or to cancel the file install.
  5. Wait for the tape screen. The workshop reconnects by itself, checks the installed files and settings, and then says Update installed and verified. Check later stops waiting without cancelling the installation. The page remembers an unfinished update if you reload it, and until the result is known you can't edit plugins or use Undo.

Review changes covers your workshop choices and your own plugins together. When no firmware upgrade is needed, it sends only the files that changed and doesn't ask for the original R246. Unticking a workshop plugin removes its file from the OP-1 on the next update.

If a firmware upgrade is needed, the review says so before you send, and the upgrade and the plugin changes go over together. The page asks for the original op1_246.op1 when it needs it; the file is processed in your browser and isn't saved. A full update can take a minute or two after the transfer. You don't need Format drive. If your OP-1 has an older version, you may have to press Review changes once more after the first update to install your own plugins.

The progress bar goes through preparing, transfer, confirmation, restart and verification. A transfer at 100 % means the files arrived, not that the installation is finished. If USB is interrupted, reconnect and press Resume update; the page checks again before it carries on. If verification can't finish, Retry verification reads the OP-1 without sending anything again and names any file that failed or doesn't match. If you cancelled with , press Review changes to prepare the update again. Use a firmware file instead is there for the first install and for recovery from the boot menu.

Your own plugins

Want to build one? Start with Make your own plugin.

Open Your plugins in the category list next to synths, effects and tools. Drop a .knl or .op1p file there, or choose files. Each file can be up to 1 MB. The page checks the file and keeps it in this browser. Press Review changes to check it together with your workshop choices. Your files go along in USB updates. A firmware download only holds the workshop plugins, so keep your own original files.

When you connect, the page also lists uploaded plugins that are already on the OP-1, even if they came from another browser or computer. You can remove those without having the file. Remove marks a plugin Will be removed and offers Undo. The file is deleted on the next reviewed update, after you confirm with , and the row disappears once that is verified. After you have sent an update, Remove and Undo give way to Verify first, because the file may already be gone: press Check again. If the last update was never confirmed, Cancel pending files clears the files waiting on the OP-1 so you can use Undo again. It does not undo saved on/off choices.

If an uploaded file replaces a workshop plugin that is still ticked, Remove brings the workshop version back. Untick that workshop plugin too if you want neither.

The page remembers your ticks, uploaded files, pending removals and control choices when you come back. They are stored in this browser for this site address, so clearing site data deletes them and private browsing may not keep them. If saving fails, keep the tab open. Sending is blocked when the page can't save the pending update: download the firmware instead, or fix the browser storage. If you change things in another workshop tab, reload before updating; the page won't overwrite the newer choices. Only one workshop tab can use the updater at a time. Save choices and Load choices back up your ticks only, not plugin files.

Installed files that are healthy are skipped. A workshop file reported as failed is sent again even if its checksum matches, so damaged files get repaired. The review lists new, removed, switched and older plugins one per line, each with an on/off switch. Plain updates and unchanged plugins fold into one line each. If a plugin is older than the one on the OP-1, you have to confirm it. Where two plugins use the same controls, you choose which one stays on; the other stays installed but off. Plugins that don't declare their controls give a warning you have to confirm.

File installs and deletions need on the OP-1. On/off choices are saved before that: cancels the file install but does not undo them. The review tells you when on/off choices change. The page keeps the update as pending until the OP-1 reports the matching files. Failed or skipped plugins show up in the review, and an enabled plugin that was skipped after a failed start can be retried by confirming the restart the page proposes.

A plugin runs as firmware code, so only add files from people you trust. If the OP-1 doesn't start after you add one, switch it off and on: it starts once without plugin files, and you can remove yours from the workshop.

Note

If something goes wrong, the update menu is still there: hold COM while you switch on, press and install the downloaded file as usual.

Make your first sound

  1. Press , then hold Shift and press . The synth list opens.
  2. Turn blue to wavetable (or any synth in your build) and press to load it.
  3. Play a few keys. The wave factory on screen comes alive while you play.
  4. Turn blue slowly. The sound morphs through eight wave shapes, from sine to digital.

Every synth and effect in this manual is picked the same way: Shift + for synths, Shift + for effects, Shift + for LFOs and Shift + for sequencers.

Note

The OP-1 saves a changed sound once the knobs have been still for 1.5 seconds, or when you press a key. Anything left is written when you switch off, so switch off normally instead of pulling the power right after a change.

Install the presets

Presets are not part of the firmware. They come in the preset pack and go in through disk mode.

  1. Unzip the .presets.zip file. Inside is a synth folder with one folder per engine.
  2. On the OP-1, hold Shift and press COM, then press for disk mode.
  3. Copy the folders inside synth/ into the synth/ folder on the OP-1 disk.
  4. Eject the disk. The OP-1 restarts by itself, syncs the new files ("Sync after reboot") and waits for a key press.

The presets are in the preset browser: in synth mode hold Shift and press a sound key, turn blue to the engine folder and green to the preset.

Warning

Sounds already on your OP-1 count toward the preset limit. If you go over it, the OP-1 silently drops the presets past the limit when it syncs. hundekraft always has room for 300 user synth presets and 54 sampler sounds of your own (TE's firmware stops at 33); remove sounds you no longer need before adding more.

Note

Granular presets store settings, not audio. Load or record a sample into the slot before you play them.

Go back, or get unstuck

The firmware update menu is always there, even if the OP-1 hangs on the boot screen. A normal upgrade does not require Format drive.

  • To try a different build, install it the same way: hold COM while switching on, then upload the new .op1.
  • To go back to stock, install the original op1_246.op1 the same way.
Warning

Stock firmware and older builds have room for fewer sounds: stock keeps 100 of your own synth presets and 33 of your own sampler sounds (hundekraft before 3.0.4 also 33). With more on the OP-1, the older firmware stops on the boot screen. Before going back, copy your sounds to a computer in disk mode and delete the extra ones. If it already stopped, install the newer build again, then remove sounds.

  • If the OP-1 stops on the boot screen, hold COM while switching on and install a new build.

hundekraft protects itself from broken plugin files. A file that fails its checks is skipped, and the OP-1 starts without it. If the last start didn't finish, it skips all plugin files once and tries them again the next time. A message at start says how many files it skipped, for example "1 plugin files not loaded". Plugins switched off in the workshop, including the loser of a key conflict, are left out of this warning.

A sound whose plugin isn't there anymore still loads: a missing synth plays as the stock Iter, a missing effect as the stock filter. The sound keeps its own engine and effect name, so it comes back as it was when you install the plugin again.

Warning

Stock R246 isn't as forgiving. Before going back to stock, change every saved sound that uses a synth or effect from op1r, Musica Obscura or hundekraft, or the OP-1 can stop at boot.

Factory reset (boot-menu 7) clears user sounds, tape and settings. Format drive (8) erases the filesystem and then asks for firmware to upload. Both destroy data: back up before using them. A complete firmware install replaces any unfinished USB update and its recovery state. Imported plugin files can survive a factory reset or a normal firmware install. Use the workshop’s installed-plugin review to identify and remove an unwanted file.

Synths

Pick a synth with Shift + , like a stock one. hundekraft's own synths come first, then the ones from op1r (pcppcp's firmware, which hundekraft is built on) and from ultralazr's Musica Obscura.

Note

The key-played hundekraft synths have animated screens, follow velocity, play up to six voices, and work with the OP-1’s envelope (T2) and LFO (T4). The LFO can sweep any of their four encoders. Amp instead processes live input continuously and uses T2 for its tuner; see its section below.

Wavetable

Morphing waves. Analog ladder filter.

Two slightly detuned oscillators read eight single-cycle waves and morph between them. The sound then goes through a four-pole ladder low-pass filter that can scream when you push it. Think classic analog pads and acid lines, with some vocal and digital colours on top.

Load then Shift + pick wavetable

The screen is a stack of oscilloscope traces of Wavetable's own waves, sine at the front and digital at the back. The bright green trace is the wave you play. Turn Wave and it moves through the stack, with the blue bracket marking its place. The fainter green copy beside it drifts with Detune, both traces swell as you play, and a blue dot runs along the wave. The small bars at the top right show its harmonics. Underneath, the white line is the filter. Its bend moves right as Cutoff opens, and Resonance lifts a peak with up to four red arcs over it.

Taffy

Elastic, CZ-style phase distortion.

Phase distortion in the style of Casio's CZ synths. A plain cosine is bent out of shape: the more you bend it, the brighter it gets. There is no filter. Brightness comes from the bend, so the sound stays clean and a little glassy.

Load then Shift + pick taffy

The screen is a girl blowing a bubblegum bubble. The blue outline of the bubble is the live wave: nearly round with no distortion, and more bent toward the chosen shape as Distortion goes up. The bubble grows with the level of the notes you hold and shrinks when you let go. Each note's envelope kick pushes it out a little further and makes the white sparkles flash. The red glints inside follow Resonance, and there are none at 0.

Pew

Drawbar organ. Rotary speaker.

A drawbar organ in a jazz club, played through a rotary speaker. Each encoder is a whole organ control, so every position is a sound an organist would use: blue pulls out the drawbars, green adds the percussion, white adds chorus and then drive, and orange switches the rotary speaker. The screen moves with them: the drawbars slide out, notes fly up when you play, the smoke and the lamp follow white, and the red lines spin round the cabinet with the speaker.

Amp

Your input is the instrument.

Amp turns the OP-1's live input into the instrument. The mic, line, radio or USB signal runs through a compressor, a tone control and an amp with a speaker cabinet. No key needs to be held. It is inspired by the OP-1 Field's live-input instrument.

To use it:

  1. Tick Amp in the workshop's Synths list, then install it (a full firmware or Review changes).
  2. On the OP-1, press , hold Shift and press . Turn blue to amp and press to load it.
  3. Press the input key to switch monitoring on. Hold Shift and press the input key to choose mic, line, radio or USB.
  4. Feed audio into that source and raise blue Volume.

Compression; 0 is off, higher values tame peaks

Tone

Warm to bright; the middle is neutral and the screen shows 0 there

Saturation through the voice's amp and cabinet, with a gate that cuts hiss between notes; 0 is clean

Press blue to turn it into Voice: a box on the left lights up with a picture of the amp, and each click of blue steps to the next one: Clean, Crunch, Lead, Bass or Fuzz. Each voice has its own tone stack, speaker cabinet and amount of drive, and the louder you play, the more it squashes, like a valve amp. Voice only changes the sound when Drive is above 0. Press blue again to get Volume back. Older sounds use Crunch.

T1 shows the four values and the input as a moving line. T2 is a tuner for one sustained note. The needle leans left into red when the note is flat and right into blue when it is sharp, and stands in the green middle when it is in tune. Under the note name you see how many cents off it is. Resampling the master (MASTER as the sampler source) is silent while Amp is the synth.

Play and sequence

Tools that change what your keys play, plus sequencers, drum+ and a four-in-one LFO. Chord and Scale work with hand-played notes and sequencers. They keep their own pages: Chord is on Shift + , then ; Scale is on the synth's page while holding Shift. The sequencer browser stays under Shift + .

Randomiser

Keep the instrument. Find another sound inside it.

On a synth page, hold Shift and press to make a new variation of the current sound. The engine stays the same; its knobs, envelope, effect and LFO can change. Randomiser chooses from the effects and LFOs available on this OP-1. MIDI LFO is excluded.

  • Shift + : step forward through existing variations, or generate a new one at the newest step. The panel says RANDOM.
  • Shift + : step back. The panel says MODNAR. At the original sound, another press does nothing.

Each of the eight synth slots keeps its original sound and six most recent variations in memory. Turning knobs before stepping preserves those edits in that history entry. Changing the engine starts a new history for the slot. History is cleared on restart; the current sound uses the normal slot saving, and you can save a favourite with the usual held sound key.

Changing a variation stops any held notes. The shortcuts are inactive in drum/tape mode and while the sampler or text editor is open. Randomising a sampler changes settings, not its recorded audio.

Random results can be bright, loud or expensive to play: try fewer notes or a lighter effect if audio peaks. Randomiser does not guarantee that a heavy engine/effect/LFO combination fits the audio budget. Leaving Randomiser out removes its shortcuts, not sounds already made with it.

Degree mode

Each key plays the chord that belongs to it in the chosen scale. In C major, C plays C major, D plays D minor, G plays G major, and so on. The key you press is always the bass note, so higher keys give higher chords, and the octave keys work as usual.

  • Hold Shift while you press a key to play a dominant 7th on it. On the fourth degree Shift swaps minor and major (iv and IV), and on the raised fourth it plays a half-diminished chord.
  • Keys outside the scale play what green push chose. LEAD plays a dominant 7th that leads into the next scale chord, PASS a diminished 7th passing chord, and BORR a borrowed chord (for some keys a slash chord, such as D/F# in major).
  • blue push adds colour to the scale's triads in degree mode: sus4, sus2, the scale's own 7th, or add9. Shift chords, LEAD and PASS chords and step mode stay as they are.

Step mode

Turn orange to STEP. Now every key press plays the next chord of the progression on white. The key you press only sets the register. Hold Shift and press a key to step back.

The progressions are pop, 50s, jazz, andalus, blues, canon, sad, royal, rock, circle, turn, epic, creep, soul, min251 and dreamy.

Record your own progression

  1. In degree mode, push white. The page shows "rec".
  2. Play up to 16 chords. Colours and Shift chords are recorded too.
  3. Push white again. Your progression is saved as "mine".
  4. Turn orange to STEP and white to "mine" to play it back one press at a time.

In degree mode, Shift + push white clears "mine". It follows the root, so you can transpose it with blue.

Good to know

  • Chords play only in the POLY play mode. In mono, legato and unison each key plays a single note.
  • A triad uses three of the six voices and a 7th uses four. Holding several chords steals voices from each other.
  • MIDI out sends only the key you pressed, not the chord.
  • With scale mode on, keys are mapped into that scale before chord mode sees them, so out-of-scale chords can't be reached. Switch scale mode off when you use chord mode.
  • Settings and your recorded progression are saved and survive a restart.

Scale mode

Locks the keyboard to a scale, so wrong notes stop sounding.

  1. Press , then .
  2. Hold Shift. The scale view appears.
  3. Turn white to choose the key and orange to choose the scale. A message confirms it, for example "scale c major".
  4. Let go of Shift and play.

Each white key plays the note of its letter in the scale, black keys play only if they're in it. It works on everything that reaches the synth: the keyboard, MIDI in and the sequencers. While you hold Shift on the scale view, push orange to change how the keys map. A message shows the new mode:

  • Snap: a key outside the scale plays the scale note just below it.
  • White keys: the white keys play the scale's notes in order, with C as the root. Black keys are silent.

Undo and redo

Every recording can be undone, up to seven steps back: takes, punch-ins, overdubs, loop takes, lift, drop, slide (Shift + blue), split, join and the tape edits below. It works with the tape stopped or playing. Sliding a clip takes two undo steps: the first undoes the drop, the second restores the lift.

  • Hold and press to undo.
  • Hold and press to redo.

The history is saved on the OP-1, so it survives switching off. Each of the four tapes has its own. FILE counts undo history under "sounds".

Note

An eighth step drops the oldest one, and recording or editing after an undo drops the steps you could have redone. The whole history is cleared when you erase the tape, when you connect in disk mode, or when free space would drop below 24 MB ("undo off: low on space"). If you press undo while the OP-1 is still writing the last take, it says "still writing": wait a moment and try again. With nothing left, it says "nothing to undo" or "nothing to redo". A take too long to keep ("too long to undo") or an edit while an undo runs ("undo cut short") also clears the history.

  2. On the OP-1 tab (), hold Shift and press COM again. FILE opens. The same keys switch back. In CTRL, DISK or OPT, Shift + COM does what it always did; press first to reach FILE.

The reel and the list show tape (all four tapes), sounds, factory content, system, mods (plugin files), bad blocks, overhead and free space. Each has its own colour on the reel, and the list gives its size in MB or KB. Push any encoder except blue to count again. A mode key leaves COM, and COM opens on its stock screen next time.

Flash check

In FILE, push blue. The OP-1 reads every block of its storage, without changing anything, and sorts them:

CountMeans
HEALTHYRead fine
FIXEDRead with a small error that was corrected
ERRORSHas a page that couldn't be corrected
DEADAlready retired by the file system

The map below has a tiny square for every block, in rows across the screen. Blue is healthy, white fixed, red errors and pink dead. Navy blocks aren't read yet. The map fills in row by row as the scan goes, so most of it looks like solid bands of colour, and a bad block shows up as a speck. SCAN shows the progress. The check pauses (PAUSED) while the tape runs, in disk mode or when you leave the screen, and carries on when you come back. Push blue again to start a new scan, or another encoder to go back to FILE. Nothing is saved: run it again any time.

Name your sounds

  1. Hold a sound key (1 to 8) to save as usual. When the OP-1 would save, a NAME screen opens instead.
  2. Turn blue (LETTER on the screen) to move one letter, green (ROW) to move one row.
  3. Push white (ADD) to add the letter. Pushing blue adds it too. Press (DEL) to delete the last one.
  4. Push orange (OK, the red ring on the screen), or press the same sound key again, to save with that name.

Names are up to 12 characters: a to z, 0 to 9, dash, underscore and space. If the name exists, the new sound gets _1 added. Leave it empty, or press a mode key, to get the usual date-stamp name. It works for synth and drum sounds.

Safer recording

Stock firmware can wrongly retire good flash blocks while you record, which shrinks your free space for good. This firmware avoids that. The flash check shows the condition of your storage. Blocks that were retired earlier stay unavailable until a format. Formatting erases everything, so back up first.

On tapes 2 to 4, REC shows "flash full" and won't start with less than 32 MB free, because a take there needs new space. If the space still runs low during a take, it stops below 8 MB free and shows "flash full, rec stopped". Everything recorded up to that point is kept. Tape 1 has its space from the start, so it always records.

While you record, the OP-1 now writes each part of the take to flash at most once a second instead of on every pass, so the screen, keys and sequencers stay smooth with heavy synths and Gin. Stopping, switching tapes, disk mode and switching off write everything first. Only a sudden power loss in the middle of a take, such as a flat battery, can lose up to the last second of it.

Resource monitor

Shift + , then . The top shows CPU, audio load, memory, disk and MIDI activity. Press again to close it. The AUDIO bar shows instrument, envelope, effect and other work, and its peak marker helps spot brief bursts of high load.

Turn blue to step through six pages on the bottom line. It wraps around, and the line starts with the page number, such as 1/6.

  1. Audio: the audio load in percent, split into INST, ENV, FX and REST.
  2. Voices: the instrument load split into SYNTH, SAMPLER and DRUM. Once the OP-1 has silenced voices to keep up, a count such as "shed 3" is added.
  3. Plugins: how many plugin files loaded, and how many were REFUSED. SKIP shows only when files were skipped.
  4. Database: DB OK, or DB ERR with a number if the sound database has a problem.
  5. Peak (PK): the slowest audio block since you opened the monitor, as a percent of the time the OP-1 has for one block, split into INST, ENV, FX and REST. Over 100 means a block ran late and you may have heard a crackle. "FX -" means no synth or drum effect ran in that block. Push any encoder to start measuring again.
  6. Min gap: the shortest time between two audio blocks starting, as a percent of normal. 100 means every block started on time; lower means the audio ran late at some point. A pair of dashes means no reading yet. Push any encoder to start again.

When the audio gets close to running late, the OP-1 first silences plugin synth voices one at a time instead of crackling. Released notes go first, quietest first, then the oldest held note. The note you just played always stays. A drum plugin that still can't keep up is muted with "Drum synth muted: overload".

If an audio overload message appears, the OP-1 may have temporarily replaced a synth or effect to keep playing. A bypassed synth falls back to Iter; an effect falls back to Filter. Restart to restore it, then reduce the load. Short peaks can still crackle before this protection acts.

USB output

Shift + , then . Turn blue to choose MASTER or MASTER + TRACKS.

USB always has six channels: main left and right on 1 and 2, and the four tape tracks on 3 to 6. With MASTER the track channels are silent. With MASTER + TRACKS they carry the four tape tracks, taken before the faders. MASTER (the default) uses less processing. On Windows, remove the old OP-1 audio device if it still shows two channels.

Settings

Shift + , then . Turn blue to set the count-in to 4, 8 or 16 beats. Turn green to switch the MIDI sequencer on or off.

MIDI control

Over USB MIDI, on the OP-1's MIDI channel, a computer or controller can press keys and turn encoders.

  • Keys: a CC number presses a key (value 64 or more) or lets go (below 64). Examples: 5 help, 6 metronome, 7 to 10 synth, drum, tape and mixer, 11 to 14 T1 to T4, 38 REC, 39 PLAY, 40 STOP, 43 Shift, 49 COM.
  • Encoders: CC 102 to 105 turn blue to orange. Values 1 to 63 turn right, 65 to 127 turn left.
  • Mixer: CC 106 to 109 set track volume, 110 to 113 pan, 114 to 117 mute, and 118 tape input level.
  • Synth knobs: CC 53 to 60 and 72 to 79, see CC automation.

MIDI sync: when the OP-1 syncs to MIDI, MIDI START restarts the pattern sequencer, Euclid and Dots so the first step lands on the master's first clock.

OP-Z controller mode is a hidden stock mode that can be switched on from a computer with SysEx. Most people won't need it.

Troubleshooting

The OP-1 hangs on the boot screen. Hold COM while switching on and install a new build. If you went back to stock or an older build, a saved sound may use an effect that firmware doesn't have; see Go back, or get unstuck.

The workshop cannot find the OP-1. Connect it by USB and wait for the tape screen before checking or sending. If you just confirmed an update, let it finish restarting. Otherwise reconnect the USB cable and try again. Use a supported desktop browser and allow MIDI access when asked.

The workshop rejects my firmware file. It only accepts the original, unmodified op1_246.op1. Get a fresh copy from Teenage Engineering.

The OP-1 says "plugin files not loaded" when it starts. One or more plugin files failed to load or were skipped after an unfinished start. Press Check again in the workshop to see which files and why. Files deliberately switched off are not counted in this warning. Send a replacement if a file is damaged. "N plugin files skipped: restart to load" means the last start ended before the OP-1 was fully up, so this start runs without plugin files. Restart once and they load again.

A sound plays as Iter, or its effect turned into the filter. Its plugin may be missing, switched off, or bypassed by audio overload protection. Press Check again in the workshop to find missing or disabled files. If an overload message appeared, restart to restore the plugin, then reduce the load; see Resource monitor. Installing a missing plugin restores sounds that use it.

The MIDI sequencer does not open with + . Check that MIDI SEQ is on in Settings, and that the MIDI sequencer shows as loaded after Check again in the workshop. If start-up skipped files, restart and let it finish. Open the tape screen first, then hold and press . Formatting is not a normal fix.

Fast chords crackle, but average audio load looks fine. Watch the peak marker in the Resource monitor: short bursts of high audio load can cause crackles. Try fewer simultaneous notes, a shorter release, or a lighter effect. Restart if an overload message bypassed a plugin.

An old Pew or Father sound sounds different. Both were rebuilt in 3.1. Pew's encoders mean other things now, so a Pew sound saved before plays differently; start from one of the new Pew presets. Father keeps its controls, but with a long Distance its tail can ring differently on some notes. Adjust and save the sound again.

A sound reports that its FX or LFO was reset. During slot loading and preset import, missing or invalid FX/LFO settings are replaced with stock defaults and switched off. Only the invalid block is reset: the other valid settings, sample and envelope are kept. Drum pad values outside their allowed ranges are corrected too. The OP-1 shows a message when it repairs a sound. Other invalid preset data may still prevent importing; keep the original file.

Undo says "still writing". The OP-1 is still saving the last take. Wait a moment and try again.

I can't switch tapes. Stop recording first, and leave disk mode. While a clipboard copy runs, a tape switch is refused: wait for the copy to finish, then try again. A new tape needs 64 MB free.

The tape doesn't record audio. The MIDI sequencer view is open, and REC belongs to it. Press to close it.

Granular makes no sound. It needs a recording first. See Granular.

Dots won't start. Switch recording off with before you turn orange. See Make Dots play.

Black keys don't play chords. Scale mode silences black keys outside its scale. Switch scale mode off to reach those chords.

The tape takes up a lot of space. An empty tape 1 takes about 240 MB. That's normal: the OP-1 sets that space aside when it makes the tape. Files you copy onto the tape disk in disk mode can grow it to 384 MB, and deleting them or erasing the tape doesn't free that space again. Neither does removing plugins, which are small. Tapes 2 to 4 also keep the space their recordings use. Only a format gives the space back, and it erases everything, so copy your tapes and sounds off first. The FILE view shows what uses the space.

Presets went missing after syncing. You went over the preset limit. hundekraft has a fixed limit of 300 user synth presets and 54 sampler sounds of your own. The sync screen says "Failed to create synth patch" for each one past the limit. Remove some sounds before syncing more.

In a room is silent. Push orange to capture a loop first. Large rooms hum very low.

My old reverb sounds wrong. If the sound was saved with a very old op1r build, Mix and Tone are swapped in Reverb. Turn them back.

Make your own plugin

Make a synth, effect or tool and share it as one .knl file. You only compile your plugin; you don’t need to build the full firmware.

Give your coding assistant the plugin development guide — llms.txt, and describe the sound or behavior you want. It includes the SDK download, templates, build commands and API reference.

To try the result, add the .knl under Your plugins in the workshop and press Review changes. Share the file with its controls, source and license information.
